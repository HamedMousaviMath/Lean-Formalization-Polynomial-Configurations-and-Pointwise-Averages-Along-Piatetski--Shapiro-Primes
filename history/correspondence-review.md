## Findings requiring catalogue/status precision

No mathematical defect was found in the checked statements reviewed. The following scope distinctions must remain explicit:

1. **Main conclusions are specifications only.** `QuantitativeRecurrence` and `PointwiseConclusion` are definitions of propositions, not proofs. `PointwiseConclusion` describes the conclusion for fixed data; it does not contain the probability, boundedness, and measurability assumptions or the uniform c-range of a fully quantified Theorem 1.2. Those hypotheses must appear in a future main theorem. `IntegerAction` itself works for an arbitrary measure, despite the comment mentioning probability. Its precise data correctly encode an invertible measurable measure-preserving integer action. No existing theorem concludes pointwise convergence for arbitrary functions/systems.
2. **The two external inputs are uninstantiated.** `ShaoSunPhaseInput` and `FiniteVNInput` are explicit propositions with strong analytic content; declaring them adds no mathematical axioms but does not verify their published provenance or the required translations. The nilsequence-to-real-phase specialization, the definition of the uniform phase supremum, and its relation to the bound-for-every-phase interface are not Lean-proved by these declarations. The catalogue should label those bridges quoted/unformalized.
3. **Integer scale specialization.** `ShaoSunPhaseInput` quantifies over natural X≥1. The paper writes a general upper cutoff X. All current applications use integer scales. If the cited source is formulated for real X, this is the appropriate integer-scale specialization; no checked theorem here claims the reverse real-cutoff extension. After shrinking σ≤1, floor-based real extension is elementary, but not implemented by this interface.
4. **Positive-degree VN specialization.** `FiniteVNInput` explicitly requires all R_i to have positive degree, whereas the displayed restatement of Theorem 1.3 only writes strict degree order. This is sufficient for every application to the actual family (and its affine shifts), whose positive degrees are assumed. The catalogue should identify it as this specialization of the cited result, not the complete theorem for every possible degree-zero input.
5. **Generic rescaling bridges.** The checked affine-configuration lemmas are stated for a generic index type, potentially including P₀=0. Their content is the exact manuscript change of variables and preservation of all distinctness, but application to the particular `Fin.cons`-based `ContainsConfiguration` predicate is not packaged into a separate theorem. Coefficient bounds and degree preservation are checked; a named complete `(C,Q)`-coefficient structure and its bundled instance are not present in these reviewed sources.

## Definitions and main-statement quantifiers

* `initialInterval N = Finset.Icc 1 N` has the paper's positive endpoints, including the empty interval at N=0.
* `IsPS c n` uses positive integer sequence indices and natural floor of the real power. For the relevant c>1 and positive k, this agrees with the nonnegative integer value of the ordinary floor. If the manuscript's N convention includes 0, the literal set S_c also contains 0; excluding it here does not affect PS primes or either Mangoldt weight, since 0 is not prime and Λ(0)=0. Do not state unrestricted literal equality of the two S_c conventions at n=0.
* `IsPSPrime c p` requires both actual `Nat.Prime p` and actual membership in this floor-power sequence. There is no auxiliary sparse prime model. Primality ensures p≥2.
* `sparseMangoldt γ n` uses exactly γ⁻¹ n^(1−γ) Λ(n) times this indicator, with sequence exponent 1/γ. The claims are only intended for γ=1/c in the positive near-1 range. The von Mangoldt prime value and n=1 cancellation are checked. A natural-index-only weight does not assert an integer extension without saying so.
* `PolynomialFamily` requires ℓ>0, zero constant coefficients, positive natural degrees, and strict increasing degree order on `Fin ℓ`. Positive degree implies nonzero polynomials, so natural degree is the same actual degree used in the paper. The manuscript's notation P_ℓ=d and P₁ force a nonempty family implicitly.
* `pattern P x t = Fin.cons x (fun i => x+P_i(t))` includes x once and then all shifted terms once. Injectivity on `Fin (ℓ+1)` gives all pairwise distinctness, including noncollision with x. `t=(p:ℤ)−1` is integer subtraction, not truncated natural subtraction. There is no extra restriction on p relative to N.
* `QuantitativeRecurrence P` chooses cstar and κ before c, and N₀ after c. Thus the c-range and density exponent are uniform in c, as required. It quantifies over every integer interval subset A and has the exact N/(log N)^κ threshold. Its N₀ is existential natural; increasing a successful threshold to at least 2 would discard totalized log/division edge conventions at N≤1 without weakening the eventual assertion. It is an unproved specification.
* `IntegerAction` has zero/addition laws and measure-preservation for each integer, hence inverses are supplied by the negative action parameter; polynomial values of either sign are supported. Orbit products use P_i(n−1), matching both averages. Both selected averages have the actual cardinality denominator. The zero-denominator convention is harmless only after eventual positivity has been proved; the source correctly does not claim that proof here.
* `PointwiseConclusion` requests a single limit function L and a common almost-everywhere set where both actual selected averages converge to L. This faithfully states equality of limits once a future theorem supplies the surrounding hypotheses.

## External-input quantifiers and the E interface

`ShaoSunPhaseInput` chooses ε and σ after the degree r but **before γ**. It chooses C only after γ and before X and the polynomial φ. Thus neither C nor σ depends on real phase coefficients, X, progression modulus, or progression length. The additional ε<1 can be obtained by shrinking any admissible positive range. This preserves the paper's intended uniformity.

`PhaseBound r M θ (M E)` bounds the unnormalized phase sums for every real polynomial of natural degree≤r. For r=d, degree≤d is the paper's degree<d+1. Positive and negative phase signs are equivalent because the class is closed under negation. For M≥1, dividing this inequality by M gives that every normalized phase correlation is≤E, and therefore its supremum is≤E. With K≥1, the power 1/K is positive, so monotonicity transfers the published bound to the E formulation. Conversely, using E equal to the finite supremum recovers the customary statement. The phase correlations are bounded because they are finite sums, and the phase norm is exactly 1. This is a valid ordinary mathematical normalization; the equivalence to a supremum has not been encoded as a separate Lean theorem.

`FiniteVNInput` chooses K after d and before the family, support constant, functions, scale, and E; C may depend on the fixed family and C₀. It does not assert a numeric upper formula for K in d, but its quantifier order provides degree-uniform existence. This corresponds to choosing a degree-only exponent in the manuscript's K≪_d1 statement; reference verification must confirm this exponent uniformity. The support requirements are closed intervals [−C₀M^d,C₀M^d] (the zero condition has strict >), functions are globally 1-bounded, θ is 1-bounded only on [M], and the normalization is exactly M^(−d−1). `f₀` is genuinely finitely supported. The sum over its support equals the unrestricted integer sum since the f₀ factor vanishes outside it. There is no replacement by a cyclic average.

## Progression restriction

The Fourier identity is for residue class 1 modulo a strictly positive modulus represented by `[NeZero Q]`. It remains correct at Q=1. The normalized character average removes a factor Q exactly; the norm estimate does not lose an extra modulus factor. The character's real phase is a(n−1)/Q with the same + sign as additive-character orthogonality.

`progressionSum_reindex_zero` includes y=0 and the representative n=1. `progressionSum_reindex` removes exactly this term under f(1)=0, which the actual discrepancy weight satisfies. All upper endpoints are QM+1, not QM. The natural-number casts in `(n−1)` are justified by n≥1 when needed.

`PhaseBound.progression` composes the arbitrary polynomial ψ with η(n)=(n−1)/Q and adds the linear residue phase. The r≥1 hypothesis ensures degree≤r after adding this linear phase. Polynomial coefficients remain arbitrary real numbers. Exact evaluation after n=Qy+1 gives ψ(y), and no subset of phases is substituted silently.

`progressionPhase_of_shaoSun` proves manuscript Lemma 2.2 under only the displayed polynomial-phase external input. It chooses the shrunken saving min(σ,1/2) **before γ**, retaining the same admissible ε. Its constant is 2C after γ and is uniform in all Q,M≥1 and every phase coefficient. Shrinking the saving is legitimate for cutoffs≥1. The endpoint shift estimate is applied with exponent 1−min(σ,1/2)∈[1/2,1), so (QM+1)^t≤2(QM)^t. This correctly discharges the manuscript's endpoint issue and covers Q=M=1 as well as large scales.

## Rescaling and affine preservation

The polynomial is defined coefficientwise over ℤ, with coefficient a_j q^(j−1) for j≥1 and zero constant. `eval_rescale_mul` explicitly assumes the original constant is zero and proves q P_q(y)=P(qy), even at q=0. The quotient corollary requires q≠0 and uses exact integer divisibility rather than replacing the construction with truncated division.

Positive degree is preserved for q≠0. The lower-coefficient bound uses q≥1 and |a_e|≥1 for the nonzero leading coefficient; this is exactly the reason a common C works for all positive moduli in the paper. The leading coefficient formula follows from the proved coefficient theorem. Nested rescaling composes q and k multiplicatively. The configuration lemmas require nonzero scaling for distinctness, prove affine membership and injectivity, and preserve the original positive-variable case as a specialization. The successive-modulus identity is exactly k P_{qk}(y)=P_q(ky); the unchanged prime parameter follows from integer associativity `(qk)y+1=q(ky)+1`. No stronger prime-distribution assumption is introduced by these algebraic lemmas.
